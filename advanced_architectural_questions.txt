ADVANCED ARCHITECTURAL QUESTIONS (ARIA PHASE 2)

Group: ONTOLOGICAL CONVERGENCE
Priority: CRITICAL
Question: Why is the Object-Centric (OCEL) schema an "Island" of support rather than the core event representation?
Context: The current orm.py defines OCELLog and OCELObjectType, but IngestionService only populates the case-centric ProcessCase model. Standard mining algorithms operate on 1:N case-hierarchies.
Concern: Transitioning to multi-object mining later will require a total abandonment of the ProcessEvent table or a massive, breaking migration. We are effectively building a legacy system for case-centric mining while "mocking" object-centricity in the schema.
Hypothesis: The OCEL tables were added as a forward-looking feature but are not yet integrated into the ingestion pipeline, leading to a latent semantic rift.

Group: SEMANTIC FIDELITY
Priority: HIGH
Question: How do we bridge the "Semantic Gap" between raw CSV headers and domain concepts without a mapping layer?
Context: IngestionService relies on string patterns (e.g., "case_id", "timestamp"). There is no formal Ontology mapping defined during upload.
Concern: As the system scales to different domains (Logistics vs. Healthcare), the lack of a semantic meta-model means the mining algorithms have no "understanding" of the data beyond its structure.
Hypothesis: The current MVP assumes the user manages semantics externally before upload, leaving the system "blind" to the business meaning of attributes.

Group: MULTI-PERSPECTIVE TENSION
Priority: MED
Question: Is the system prepared for "Path Semantics" beyond simple control-flow variants?
Context: AnalyticsService focuses on frequency, cycle time, and variants. Research suggest that neglecting causal knowledge and path semantics often leads to "Analysis Paralysis" where users see patterns but not root causes.
Concern: By optimizing for "Happy Path" discovery, we may be architecturally ignoring the "Why" behind the "What."
Hypothesis: The current architecture is optimized for visualization and descriptive analytics, but its data structure is too shallow for deep causal inference.

Group: DATA DETERMINISM
Priority: MED
Question: Should the system enforce sequence indices at the ingestion layer to prevent timestamp collisions?
Context: High-frequency logs often produce identical timestamps for concurrent events. ProcessCase.events.order_by("timestamp") is non-deterministic in these cases.
Concern: In social network discovery or handover analysis, a few milliseconds of jitter or identical timestamps can flip the "Source" and "Target," leading to invalid organizational insights.
Hypothesis: Reliance on wall-clock time as a sequence identifier is a structural liability that will manifest as "ghost" bottlenecks and incorrect variant discovery.

Group: ARCHITECTURAL OBSERVABILITY
Priority: MED
Question: What is the "Recovery Path" for a partially ingested Multi-Stage Log?
Context: Ingestion is an implicit state. If a multi-million row ingest fails halfway, the database is left in an inconsistent state (an EventLog with missing ProcessCases).
Concern: There is no formal "INGESTION_PENDING" or "DIRTY" flag in the EventLog orm model to prevent analytics from running on partial data.
Hypothesis: The system assumes "Atomic Success" or "Silent Failure," lacking the robust state management required for enterprise-grade data engineering.

Group: PROCESS AGING (CONCEPT DRIFT)
Priority: HIGH
Question: Does our AI know when it's "getting old"?
Context: The current prediction system trains on a fixed history. If the business process changes (e.g., new software is deployed), the AI will keep making predictions based on the old "wrong" way.
Concern: We have no automatic "Drift Detection." The AI doesn't raise a flag saying, "Hey, the data I'm seeing today doesn't look like what I learned from."
Hypothesis: We currently rely on the user to manually retraining models, which is risky for dynamic environments. We've missed a "Check for Change" step in the analysis loop.

Group: DATA PRIVACY & GDPR
Priority: CRITICAL
Question: Are we unintentionally creating a "Surveillance Machine"?
Context: The ingestion service stores real names and exact timestamps of employees (Resources) without any masking or encryption.
Concern: In many regions, storing raw employee performance data at this level of detail without anonymization is a major legal risk. There is no feature to "Mask Names" during upload.
Hypothesis: The system was built for clinical accuracy but lacks the "Privacy Filters" needed to protect the people being analyzed.

Group: PREDICTIVE BIAS
Priority: MED
Question: Is the AI playing favorites?
Context: Our predictive models check for "Accuracy" but not for "Fairness." It might be better at predicting performance for specific teams or shifts while being completely wrong for others.
Concern: Decisions made based on biased AI (e.g., predicting a delay for a specific resource) can lead to unfair management actions.
Hypothesis: We have no safety-checks to ensure the predictions are equally reliable across all groups (Resources, Regions, etc.).

Group: CAUSALITY VS. CORRELATION
Priority: HIGH
Question: Are we fixing the "Symptom" or the "Disease"?
Context: Our bottleneck detection uses a simple score based on wait times. It tells us "what is slow," but it doesn't prove "why it is slow."
Concern: In process mining, two things often look related but aren't. If we recommend a fix based on a mere correlation, we might waste the user's money without fixing the real problem.
Hypothesis: We need "Causal Models" that can simulate what would happen if we actually changed the process, rather than just pointing at red dots on a map.

Group: REAL-TIME GAP
Priority: MED
Question: Is this a "Dashboard of the Past" or a "Cockpit for the Present"?
Context: Every service (Mining, Analytics, Prediction) works in "Batch Mode"—reading a whole file at once. There is no support for "Streaming" data (processing events as they happen).
Concern: Modern businesses need to react *now*. By the time a user uploads a file and runs an analysis, the problem might have already cost them thousands of dollars.
Hypothesis: Our architecture is designed for "After-the-fact" audits, not for "Live-Action" management. Transitioning to real-time will require a major rework of the data pipeline.

Group: THE "FLATTENING TAX"
Priority: HIGH
Question: Why do we keep throwing away our best data?
Context: We have "Object-Centric" support, but every time we want to run a standard analysis, we have to "flatten" the data (squashing it back into a simple list).
Concern: Object-Centric data is rich—it knows how orders, items, and shipments all dance together. "Flattening" it destroys these relationships. It's like having a 3D movie but only being able to watch it in 2D.
Hypothesis: We are paying a "Complexity Tax" by supporting OCEL without truly committing to it as our primary data structure.

Group: MULTI-LEVEL BLINDNESS
Priority: HIGH
Question: Can we see the "Tree" or just the "Leaves"?
Context: Our mining algorithms assume every process is flat. In the real world, an "Order" might contain 5 "Items," each with its own "Shipment" lifecycle.
Concern: We Currently can't handle "Multi-level" processes. This leads to "Data Divergence"—where statistics get inflated because we don't know that 5 shipment events belong to 1 order case.
Hypothesis: Our system is built for simple linear flows and will struggle with the complex "Spaghetti" of modern supply chains.

Group: ACTIONLESS WORKFLOWS
Priority: MED
Question: Is this an "Observer" or a "Doer"?
Context: Our workflow service can detect problems (step: 'conformance'), but it has no way to fix them. There are no "Webhook" or "Trigger" steps.
Concern: A workflow that only tells you "something is wrong" is only half-useful. For enterprise-grade automation, the system needs to be able to "Push" a notification or trigger an RPA bot automatically.
Hypothesis: We have a "Passive Architecture." We've built a great telescope, but we haven't built the steering wheel.

Group: LINEAGE AMNESIA
Priority: MED
Question: Where did this data actually come from?
Context: When a user filters a log, we create a new "Filtered Log." However, we don't store the "Recipe" (the exact steps used to filter it) in a way that is easily reversible or auditable.
Concern: If a user sees a strange insight in a filtered log, they have no easy way to "Trace Back" to the raw data to see if the filter itself caused a bias.
Hypothesis: We are missing a "Data Provenance" layer. In an audit scenario, we might struggle to prove the integrity of our derived insights.

Group: THE TASK/PROCESS GAP
Priority: MED

Concern: Often, the "Bottleneck" isn't the software—it's the user struggling with a confusing UI. Without task mining, we are blind to the "Shadow Process" happening on the desktop.
Hypothesis: To be "World-Class," we eventually need to bridge the gap between "System Logs" and "Human Actions."
